# Title and snippet-copy protocol

Use this protocol when a brief or draft includes title-link inputs or a meta description.

## Inputs

Require the page role, primary need/query family, locale, visible page content, claim ledger, approved brand voice, and material qualifiers. A title or description must not claim a feature, price, location, availability, expertise, review, or result that the visible page and approved facts do not support.

## Title candidate

Write a descriptive, concise page title that makes the primary page identity and task clear. Keep it aligned with the visible main heading and page language, distinguish it from sibling pages, and use brand text economically. Do not create half-empty, boilerplate-only, stale, sensational, or keyword-repeated titles.

## Meta-description candidate

Write a page-specific summary that identifies the page, communicates the useful outcome, and includes a truthful differentiator or concrete detail. Add a material qualifier when it changes the searcher's decision. Prefer one or two natural sentences or an equally readable structured summary; product and database pages may use accurate programmatic fields.

Rewrite a candidate when it is only a label, generic boilerplate, duplicated across unrelated pages, a keyword list, unsupported marketing copy, or too short to explain why the result fits the query. Do not add filler to reach a fixed character count. Google may truncate or replace the description for a query; the goal is to supply an accurate, useful candidate and matching visible content.

## Delivery

Return the exact title and description, source facts, target URL/query family, uniqueness population, reviewer, and rendered/raw verification. For scaled generation, specify null handling, escaping, field order, duplicate detection, representative sparse/outlier samples, and a safe fallback that remains page-specific.
