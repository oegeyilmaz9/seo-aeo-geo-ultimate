# Corpus propagation contract

Before changing a scaled system, identify its canonical URL inventory and every surface that publishes, links to, describes, or submits that inventory.

For each record bind the source identity, canonical owner, search status, locale, detail route, search/browse result, hub/taxonomy membership, discovery links, sitemap membership, hreflang cluster, and optional maintained `llms.txt` inclusion. Add feed, schema, redirect, and provider-notification surfaces when the product actually uses them.

Apply one decision consistently across all affected surfaces. Canonical-owner pages must not disappear from discovery or remain excluded from the sitemap by an obsolete editorial flag. Redirecting duplicate sources must not remain in the sitemap or claim themselves as canonical. Hreflang alternates must resolve to compatible canonical/indexable targets. An optional `llms.txt` guide follows the canonical public source set and never overrides robots, canonical, or indexability policy.

Verify a shared template, resolver, sitemap builder, or corpus rule against the entire affected route class or an explicitly justified closed population. A small illustrative sample can diagnose a problem; it cannot close a corpus-wide acceptance criterion.
