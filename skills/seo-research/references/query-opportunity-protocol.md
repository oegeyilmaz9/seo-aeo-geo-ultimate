# Query opportunity protocol

Use this protocol to decide which query families deserve page ownership and work now. “Top opportunity” has two meanings that must remain visible: the strongest evidence-backed nearer-term gain and the relevant high-volume term whose leading positions would establish category leadership. The largest volume number is not automatically the best short-term move, but difficult head terms cannot disappear from an ambitious growth plan. Leading positions are an explicit objective, never a placement guarantee.

## Required ambition contract

Every broad organic-growth slate must contain both:

1. a **nearer-term capture track** for defend, near-win, snippet/CTR, consolidation, and justified coverage work; and
2. a **head-term leadership track** for every owner-declared relevant high-volume query family plus at least one highest-relevant-demand family found for the declared market.

If reliable demand data is unavailable, record the head-term track as a required research dependency rather than omitting it. A current authority, content, architecture, brand, or visibility gap changes the staged plan and investment; it is not grounds to silently accept a peripheral position. `Defer` is allowed only for an explicit owner decision, genuine reader/product-intent mismatch, or a prohibited/misleading target. Cost, difficulty, weak authority, or current absence alone do not qualify.

## Evidence layers

1. **Owner intent.** Preserve the market, locale, audience, products/topics, and explicitly desired queries. Do not silently replace a strategic query because another phrase has more estimated volume.
2. **First-party visibility.** When authorized Search Console or Bing data exists, keep query, canonical page, country, device, search type, window, clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position together. Record anonymized-query, truncation, aggregation, and export limitations. Average position is an aggregate, not an exact current rank.
3. **Demand evidence.** Preserve provider, method, date, locale, and whether volume/difficulty is observed or estimated. Unknown demand remains unknown.
4. **Dated result-set evidence.** For each serious candidate, inspect the current locale/device result set. Record dominant intent, page/result types, freshness, source/authority expectations, visible title/snippet patterns, and representative competitors. Do not copy competitor text or treat one snapshot as a stable formula.
5. **Site fit.** Bind the query family to an existing canonical owner, a justified new page, consolidation work, or no-action. Review content evidence, internal discovery, authority/source support, technical eligibility, and conversion/business fit.
6. **Leadership gap.** For each head term, compare the intended owner with representative leading results across intent/format, original usefulness, product or topic depth, source evidence, brand/entity trust, referring-source authority, internal architecture, media/search presentation, and technical eligibility. Preserve gaps separately; do not blend them into a difficulty score.

## Opportunity lanes

- **Defend:** the site already has strong relevant visibility; preserve intent match and monitor meaningful loss.
- **Near win:** an owned, relevant page already earns meaningful impressions outside the desired leading positions and has a concrete content, presentation, authority, or architecture gap.
- **Snippet/CTR:** the page is shown for the right query but its title, snippet inputs, or visible promise poorly represents the result. Compare against its own position/device/query context rather than a universal CTR threshold.
- **Coverage gap:** validated demand and reader need exist, but no suitable canonical owner exists.
- **Consolidate:** multiple pages compete for the same need and evidence supports one owner.
- **Strategic build:** business/reader value is high but current visibility or authority is weak; require a staged content and authority plan rather than calling it a quick win.
- **Head-term leadership:** a relevant high-volume category query is a required strategic target. Name the canonical owner, intended leading-position objective, competitive gap, supporting cluster/assets, authority and architecture work, milestone evidence, and investment/owner dependencies. It runs alongside quick wins rather than waiting for them to finish.
- **Defer:** an explicit owner decision, genuine intent/product mismatch, or prohibited/misleading claim makes the query unsuitable. Missing demand evidence becomes a research dependency, and a difficult but relevant head term stays in `Head-term leadership`.

## Prioritization

Assign `now`, `next`, `later`, or `defer` only after reviewing owner intent, intent fit, first-party evidence, demand provenance, current result-set expectations, page ownership, evidence/authority gap, implementation cost, and risk. Show those dimensions separately. The head-term owner and foundational work must enter `now` or `next` even when the leading-position objective is long-term; `later` may hold later stages, not the entire ambition. A sorting heuristic may create a candidate slate, but it cannot be reported as a universal keyword score.

For a head term, define an explicit target outcome and observable progression. Unless the owner supplies a different ambition, use top-3 as the leadership target and position 1 as an explicit stretch objective; track indexed/eligible ownership, query impressions, and entry into top-20, top-10, and top-3 visibility bands for the pinned locale/device alongside clicks and CTR. Treat Search Console average position as aggregated evidence and confirm important checkpoints with dated result-set observations; bands and position 1 are goals and measurements, not forecasts or guarantees.

Select one primary need/query family per page and natural supporting language that belongs to the same reader task. Use prominent, descriptive locations where useful—title, main heading, visible answer/content, link text, and relevant alt text—without exact-match repetition or keyword stuffing.

## Output row

For each opportunity return: query/need family; locale/device; evidence dates; first-party query/page metrics or `unavailable`; demand source/status and demand tier; current owner and coverage state; result-set intent/page types; opportunity lane and ambition track; priority; intended outcome; representative leading-result gap; required content/product/technical/architecture/authority work; supporting cluster/assets; staged milestones; acceptance evidence; limitations; and refresh trigger.
